Starting a business in Nepal has never been more accessible. With recent digital reforms and streamlined processes, entrepreneurs can now register their companies faster than ever before. Whether you're a local entrepreneur with a groundbreaking idea or a foreign investor eyeing Nepal's growing market, understanding the legal framework and registration process is your foundation for success.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about business law and company registration in Nepal as of 2026, including step-by-step procedures, costs, required documents, and expert tips to navigate the system efficiently.

Understanding Nepal's Business Law Framework

Nepal's business environment is governed primarily by the Companies Act 2063 (2006), which provides the legal foundation for all corporate entities operating in the country. The Office of the Company Registrar (OCR) serves as the central regulatory body overseeing company formations, compliance, and corporate governance.

The legal framework has undergone significant modernization in recent years, with the government implementing business-friendly reforms aimed at reducing bureaucratic hurdles. The ambitious goal is to cut company setup time to under three days by late 2026 through complete online processing — a remarkable improvement from the weeks-long process of the past.

Key Regulatory Bodies

Office of the Company Registrar (OCR): The primary authority for company registration, maintaining corporate records, and ensuring compliance with the Companies Act.

Department of Industry (DOI): Handles foreign investment approvals and issues licenses for specific industries. Foreign investors must obtain DOI clearance before initiating company registration.

Inland Revenue Department (IRD): Manages tax registration including Permanent Account Number (PAN) and Value Added Tax (VAT) registration.

Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB): Regulates financial institutions and oversees monetary policies. Companies in banking, insurance, and financial services require NRB approval.

Why Company Registration Matters

Legal Recognition and Protection

A registered company gains official recognition as a distinct legal entity, separate from its owners. This separation provides limited liability protection, meaning your personal assets remain protected if the business faces financial difficulties or legal disputes.

Access to Financial Services

Banks and financial institutions in Nepal only extend credit facilities, business loans, and corporate banking services to registered entities. Your Certificate of Incorporation serves as proof of legitimacy when opening corporate accounts or seeking financing.

Government Contracts and Tenders

Many lucrative opportunities, particularly government contracts and international tenders, require companies to present valid registration certificates. Registration also enables participation in public procurement processes.

Tax Compliance and Credibility

Proper registration facilitates seamless PAN and VAT registration, making tax compliance straightforward. More importantly, it builds credibility with customers, suppliers, and partners who prefer dealing with formally recognized businesses.

Foreign Investment Opportunities

For businesses seeking foreign investment or planning international expansion, company registration is non-negotiable. Registered companies can legally receive foreign direct investment and establish joint ventures with international partners.

Types of Business Entities in Nepal

Private Limited Company

The most popular choice among startups and small to medium enterprises. This structure allows 2 to 101 shareholders, with restrictions on public share trading. Approximately 90% of new businesses in Nepal opt for this structure due to its simplified compliance requirements and tax benefits.

Public Limited Company

Designed for larger corporations planning to raise capital through public share offerings, public limited companies can list their shares on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE). This structure requires a minimum of seven shareholders and comes with stricter regulatory oversight.

One Person Company (OPC)

Introduced in 2025, the One Person Company provision revolutionized solo entrepreneurship in Nepal. A single individual can now establish a company with limited liability protection — previously impossible under Nepali law. Ideal for freelancers, consultants, and solo founders.

Branch Office

Foreign companies expanding into Nepal can establish branch offices to conduct business operations. However, branch offices cannot engage in manufacturing activities and face certain operational restrictions.

Partnership Firms

While not governed by the Companies Act, partnership firms remain a common business structure, particularly for professional services. Partnerships offer no limited liability protection, making them less attractive for ventures requiring significant capital investment.

The 2026 Registration Process: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Reserve Your Company Name (1–3 Days)

Visit the OCR e-Services Portal and propose three unique names in order of preference. Your names should include appropriate suffixes like "Pvt. Ltd." for private companies. The OCR typically responds within 1–3 days via email. The reservation fee ranges from NPR 100 to 500 depending on the company type.

Step 2: Create Your OCR Account and Obtain Digital Signature

Register on the OCR's New Registration Portal using your email address, citizenship number, and mobile number. You'll need a digital signature to authenticate your document uploads and submissions.

Step 3: Prepare Required Documents

  • Registration Application (Anusuchi 1) — formal application with NPR 5 government stamp
  • Memorandum of Association (Niyamawali — Anusuchi 3) — outlines company objectives, authorized capital, and shareholding pattern
  • Articles of Association (Prabandha Patra — Anusuchi 2) — internal bylaws governing company operations
  • Identity Documents — citizenship certificates (Nepali) or passports (foreign)
  • Mutual Agreement — for companies with multiple shareholders
  • DOI Approval Letter — required for foreign investors
  • Lease Agreement — proof of registered office address
  • Power of Attorney — if authorizing someone else to handle registration

Step 4: Submit Your Online Application

Log into your OCR account and upload all required documents. The OCR reviews applications within 15 days for companies with only Nepali promoters and 30 days for those with foreign investors.

Step 5: Pay Registration Fees

Registration fees are calculated based on your company's authorized capital. For authorized capital up to NPR 1 lakh, the fee is NPR 1,000. Companies with NPR 1–10 lakh capital pay between NPR 2,000 and 5,000. Payment can be made through designated bank accounts or to the Nepal Rastra Bank's government revenue account.

Step 6: Receive Your Certificate of Incorporation

Upon approval, you'll receive your digitally signed Certificate of Incorporation along with a unique company registration number. This certificate is your company's legal proof of existence. If your application is rejected, you can fix the problems and resubmit without paying additional fees.

Step 7: Complete Tax Registration

Within days of receiving your incorporation certificate, apply for your Permanent Account Number (PAN) through the Inland Revenue Department's online portal at ird.gov.np. If your projected annual turnover exceeds NPR 50 lakh, you must also register for Value Added Tax (VAT).

Step 8: Post-Registration Compliance

Within three months of incorporation, formally notify the OCR of your registered office address. Within one month of capital subscription, file your shareholder register. When appointing directors, notify the OCR within seven days. Annual returns are mandatory — failing to file can result in penalties and even company dissolution.

Common Challenges and How to Avoid Them

Incomplete Documentation: This remains the leading cause of delays. Double-check that every required document is present, properly formatted, and signed before submission.

Name Conflicts: Conduct thorough searches before submitting your name reservation. Consider having backup names ready in case your first choice is too similar to existing companies.

Foreign Investment Hurdles: Foreign investors should begin the DOI approval process early, as this pre-registration requirement can take 1–2 weeks.

Regulated Industry Requirements: Companies in banking, insurance, hydropower, and telecommunications need additional licenses and approvals from sector-specific authorities. Factor these extra steps into your timeline.

Technical Issues: While the online system is generally reliable, maintain backup copies of all documents and take screenshots of successful submissions.

Special Considerations for Foreign Investors

Foreign investment in Nepal is welcomed in most sectors, with ownership limits typically allowing up to 100% foreign equity in industries like information technology, tourism, manufacturing, and professional services. However, certain strategic sectors have restrictions — banking, financial services, media, and security services may have foreign ownership caps.

Foreign investors should also understand repatriation policies, which allow profits to be transferred abroad after paying applicable taxes. Maintaining proper documentation of foreign investment sources is crucial for smooth repatriation later.

Timeline Expectations

Under optimal conditions with complete documentation, the entire process from name reservation to certificate receipt typically takes 7–10 business days. Planning for 15–20 days provides a comfortable buffer for unexpected issues.

Foreign investment companies should allow 3–4 weeks due to the additional DOI approval requirement. The government's target of reducing registration time to under three days by late 2026 through full automation promises even faster processing in the near future.

Conclusion

Company registration in Nepal has evolved from a bureaucratic maze into a manageable, largely digital process. With proper preparation, accurate documentation, and realistic timeline expectations, you can establish your legal business entity efficiently.

Nepal's improving business environment, strategic location between major Asian economies, and young, growing workforce make it an increasingly attractive destination for entrepreneurs. Your registered company becomes your vehicle for participating in this dynamic economy.
